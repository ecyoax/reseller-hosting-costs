## What reseller hosting is, in plain terms

Reseller hosting is wholesale/retail arbitrage built on someone else's infrastructure, and the day-to-day flow is simpler than the marketing makes it sound:

1. You sign up for a reseller plan that includes WHM (WebHost Manager).
2. Inside WHM, you create the hosting packages you want to sell — say a "Starter" with 5 GB storage and 50 GB bandwidth, and a "Pro" with 20 GB and no meter.
3. A billing platform such as WHMCS automates signups, invoicing, renewals, and account provisioning.
4. Your client logs into a cPanel wearing your brand. The parent provider's name never appears. That's the "white-label" part.

One distinction worth thirty seconds, because the two get confused constantly: an affiliate refers customers to a hosting company and collects a commission. A reseller owns the client relationship and the recurring revenue. That's why reseller hosting behaves like an actual micro-business — you're on the hook for tier-1 support and billing, and you also keep every renewal.

Then there's the naming trap. WHM is the administrative back end behind cPanel — the tool where you create, suspend, and configure the individual accounts you sell. WHMCS is a separate product that automates billing and support workflows. Similar names, completely different jobs: you need WHM to run the accounts, and most resellers who get past ten clients add WHMCS so they stop invoicing people by hand.

## Refund windows: worth reading before you prepay

Refund windows in this market run from 30 days to 180 days, and the differences matter most when a plan wants three years of money up front. Thirty days is the default — KnownHost, NameHero, Stablepoint, and most of the field sit there. StableHost gives 45. Krystal and Kualo stretch to 60. InMotion offers a full 90-day guarantee, which pairs sensibly with its higher renewal prices, and MochaHost goes all the way to 180 days on its Affogato reseller plans.

One condition with teeth: Namecheap's 30-day guarantee applies only to first-time reseller customers. Create an account, cancel, and sign up again later, and you're ineligible for a refund — the same applies to anyone opening a second account. Fine print like that is exactly why the refund window belongs next to the price in every comparison you run.

## How to start a reseller hosting business, step by step

The setup takes an afternoon; finding clients is the actual work. The setup part looks like this:

1. **Pick a plan sized to your first year, not your five-year plan.** Twenty-five small client sites fit inside a 50 GB starter tier if each stays lean. The gap between $4.99 and $17.99/mo buys a lot of runway while you prove demand, and unused headroom never pays you back.
2. **Build your own website first.** Any CMS works. This is your storefront, and it doubles as proof that you can run a website before someone pays you to run theirs.
3. **Define your packages inside WHM.** Storage, bandwidth, email accounts — per plan. Most resellers start with two or three tiers, mirroring what the big hosts sell, just with your branding and your prices.
4. **Put billing on rails with WHMCS.** Signups, invoices, renewals, provisioning. Manual billing works for exactly three clients, and then it starts eating weekends.
5. **White-label the details.** Private nameservers on your domain, your logo inside cPanel, a support email at your domain. Done properly, clients have no way to tell who the upstream provider is — and no reason to go around you.
6. **Only then, go find clients.** Existing web design or development clients are the natural first ten. Hosting attaches easily to work you're already doing; it's a much harder sell cold.

## Reseller hosting FAQ

### What's the difference between WHM and WHMCS?

WHM is the administrative back end of cPanel, where you create and manage the individual hosting accounts you resell. WHMCS is a separate billing and automation platform that handles invoices, renewals, and provisioning. You eventually need both — WHM to run accounts, WHMCS to stop managing money by hand — but they're different products doing different jobs.

### Will my clients know I'm reselling someone else's hosting?

No, provided you switch on the white-label features: your own nameservers, your branding inside cPanel, and client communications from your own domain. The parent host stays invisible. That's the entire point of the model.

### Do I need to be technical to run reseller hosting?

Basic cPanel/WHM familiarity is enough. If you've ever run your own website's hosting, you already have most of the required skills. The parent provider handles hardware, network, and core software underneath, so infrastructure problems aren't yours to fix.

### Is reseller hosting the same as an affiliate program?

No. Affiliates send customers to a hosting company and collect a one-time commission. Resellers own the client relationship and keep the recurring revenue — which also means owning the support and billing relationship that comes with it.

### How much can a reseller actually make?

Your margin is the gap between wholesale and your retail price, minus your time. At least one provider advertises wholesale-to-retail margins of up to 70%, and the recurring nature is the real attraction: every client you sign is revenue that repeats monthly. The honest caveat is that support time is the hidden cost, and it scales with client count faster than storage does.
